You can be lonely in an empty flat or at a crowded table. The feeling is the gap between the company you have and the company you need, and that gap looks different for everyone. Writing won't replace people. It can show you what you're missing, which makes it easier to go and look for it.

## How to use these prompts

- Choose one prompt for tonight and leave the rest.
- If an answer makes you sad, stay with it for a few lines, then write down something you can see or hear in the room.
- The last group asks you to do something small. Try one of those each week.
- If loneliness has turned into hopelessness, please talk to a doctor or call a helpline as well as writing.

## What kind of lonely is it?

- When does loneliness usually arrive: a certain hour, a day, a season?
- Who or what do you miss tonight? Be as exact as you can.
- Do you want someone to talk to, someone to do things with, or someone who simply knows you're there?
- When were you last with other people and still lonely? What was missing?
- Describe the last time you felt that you belonged somewhere.

## What you tell yourself

- What does loneliness make you believe about yourself? Write the sentence down word for word.
- What facts from your life don't fit that sentence?
- If a friend were this lonely, what would you want them to know?
- What do you hide from people so they won't see that you're lonely?
- Is there a friendship you're still grieving? Tell the story of how it changed.

## Your own company

- Which parts of being alone do you like, even a little?
- Plan one evening alone that you would look forward to. What's in it?
- What did you love doing at ten years old that you could do by yourself this week?
- Which song, book or place makes you feel less alone? What does it understand about you?
- Write a letter to yourself from someone who loved you well.

## Reaching out

- Name three people you've lost touch with. What would you say to one of them in two sentences?
- Which familiar face, such as a neighbour or a barista, could you greet by name?
- What invitation have you been waiting for? Could you be the one who makes it?
- Where do people who care about the same things as you meet? Write down one place and one date.
- Who might be lonely near you, and what small thing could you do for them?
