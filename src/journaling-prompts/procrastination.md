Procrastination is rarely about laziness. Usually there is a feeling behind the delay: fear of doing it badly, boredom, not knowing where to start, or simply being tired. These prompts help you find that feeling and get moving again with a step small enough to take today.

## How to use these prompts

- Pick one prompt and write for five minutes. Set a timer if that helps.
- Choose the task you are avoiding right now and keep it in mind as you write.
- Don't polish your answers. Messy notes are fine.
- If you finish with one concrete next step, the entry did its job.

## What you are putting off

- What is the one task you have been avoiding the longest? How long has it been waiting?
- Describe the task as if you were explaining it to a stranger. Is it as big as it feels?
- What happens if you never do it at all? Write the honest answer.
- Which tasks do you finish without any trouble? What makes them different?

## Why you are avoiding it

- When you think about starting, what feeling shows up first: dread, boredom, confusion, something else?
- What are you afraid will happen if you start and it goes badly?
- Whose opinion of the finished work worries you most?
- Is part of you waiting to feel ready? What would "ready" actually look like?
- What do you do instead of the task? What do those activities give you that the task doesn't?

## Making it smaller

- Break the task into steps so small they feel almost silly. Write down the first three.
- What could you do in the next ten minutes that would count as progress?
- What would a deliberately rough first version look like?
- What information or tool are you missing? Who could you ask for it?
- Which part could you hand off, drop or do later without much loss?

## Getting started

- Pick a time today or tomorrow for the first step. Write down exactly where you will be and what you will do.
- What distraction pulls you away most often? How could you remove it for one hour?
- Who could you tell about your plan, so someone expects to hear how it went?
- How will you reward yourself after the first session, however short?

## Looking back

- Think of something you once put off and then finished. How did it feel afterwards?
- What did you learn about yourself from that time that applies now?
- If you started today, what would you like to be able to say about this task in a month?
- Write a short note to the version of you who will face this task tomorrow morning.
