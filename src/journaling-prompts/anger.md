Anger is a normal feeling, and sometimes it's telling you something important: a line was crossed, a need isn't being met. The trouble starts when it runs the show. Writing gives anger somewhere to go that isn't another person, and once it's on the page you can look at it more calmly.

## How to use these prompts

- If you're angry right now, start with the first section and write fast. Don't edit.
- Wait until you've cooled down before you try the later sections.
- Keep what you write private. This is not a draft of a message to send.
- One prompt per session is plenty.

## Getting it out

- Write down exactly what happened, as if you were telling a friend who's on your side.
- Write everything you wish you could say to the person you're angry with. Don't send it.
- On a scale of one to ten, how angry are you? Write again in an hour and see if the number changed.
- Where do you feel the anger in your body right now? Describe it in detail.

## What is underneath

- What did you need in that moment that you didn't get?
- Is there hurt, fear or embarrassment beneath the anger? Name it.
- Which of your values did this situation go against?
- Does this remind you of something older, from earlier in your life?
- What part of the situation was in your control, and what part wasn't?

## Seeing the other side

- What might the other person say if they told the story?
- Is there a less hostile explanation for what they did? Write one, even if you don't believe it yet.
- Have you ever done something similar to someone else? What was going on for you then?

## Patterns

- What situations, people or times of day tend to set off your anger?
- How do tiredness, hunger or stress affect your temper?
- What do you usually do when you get angry: shout, go silent, leave, something else?
- What has your anger cost you in the past year?

## Next time

- What is the earliest warning sign that your anger is building?
- Write down three things you could do in the first minute next time: a breath, a walk, a sentence you can say.
- If you need to raise this with the person, how could you say it calmly and clearly? Draft one sentence.
- What would you like the people close to you to say about how you handle anger a year from now?
- What helps you calm down fastest? Write a short list to come back to.
- Write one kind sentence to yourself about how hard you're trying.
