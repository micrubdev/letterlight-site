Writing a worry down won't make it disappear. It does move it from your head onto the page, where you can look at it instead of spinning inside it. That alone often helps.

## How to use these prompts

- Pick one prompt, and write for five or ten minutes. Stop when the timer goes, even mid-sentence.
- Don't edit or reread straight away. Messy is fine.
- If writing about a worry makes it louder, switch to one of the prompts under "Coming back to now".
- If anxiety is stopping you from working, sleeping or eating, talk to a doctor too. Journaling helps; it's not a replacement for care.

## Getting it out of your head

- What is the worry that keeps coming back today? Write it in one sentence, then in as much detail as you need.
- What's the worst you imagine happening? Now write the most likely outcome, and then the best.
- What would you tell a friend who came to you with exactly this worry?
- Which of today's worries are about the past, which about the future, and which about right now?
- What are you afraid people will think of you? How do you know that's what they think?
- List everything on your mind, big and small, without sorting it.

## Sorting what you can control

- Draw a line down the page. On one side, what you can control in this situation. On the other, what you can't.
- What is one small step you could take in the next 24 hours on the "can control" side?
- What are you trying to control that isn't really yours to control?
- If this problem were solved, what would be different tomorrow morning?
- What information do you actually need, and how could you get it, instead of guessing?
- What's a decision you've been putting off? What would happen if you made it today?

## Coming back to now

- Name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch, two you can smell, one you can taste.
- Describe the room you're in as if to someone who has never been there.
- How does your body feel right now, from head to feet? Where are you holding tension?
- What is one thing that went fine today, however small?
- Write about a place where you feel safe. Fill in the details: light, sounds, temperature.
- Breathe in for four counts and out for six, five times. Then write one sentence about how your body feels now.

## Looking at the pattern

- When did you first notice feeling anxious today? What was happening just before?
- Think of a time you were very worried and things turned out all right. What helped you get through it?
- What usually makes your anxiety worse: caffeine, scrolling, too little sleep, certain people? What usually makes it better?
- What does your anxiety seem to be trying to protect you from?
- If your anxiety had a voice, what would it be saying to you? What would you like to say back?
- What would you do this week if you weren't afraid?
- Finish this sentence five different ways: "Right now, I'm okay because…"
