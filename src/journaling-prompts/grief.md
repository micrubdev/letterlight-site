Grief does not follow stages in order, and there is no right amount to write. Some days you will fill pages. Some days one line is all you have, and one line counts.

## How to use these prompts

- Pick one prompt, not all of them. Set a timer for ten minutes if that helps you stop.
- Write by hand if you can. It's slower, and slower is often better here.
- You don't have to reread what you wrote. You don't have to show anyone.
- If a prompt hurts too much today, skip it. It will still be here.

## In the first weeks

- What happened today that you wanted to tell them about?
- Write down the last ordinary conversation you remember having with them.
- What are people saying to you that helps? What are they saying that doesn't?
- Which hour of the day is hardest right now, and what might make it a little easier?
- What have you not let yourself feel yet, because you're afraid of how big it is?
- Who has shown up for you in a way you didn't expect?

## Remembering them

- Describe their hands, their laugh, or the way they said your name.
- What is a small habit of theirs that used to annoy you and that you'd give anything to see again?
- Write about a place that belongs to the two of you.
- What did they teach you, on purpose or by accident?
- Tell a story about them that most people don't know.
- If you could keep one object of theirs forever, which would it be, and why that one?

## Things left unsaid

- Write them a letter. Start with "I never told you…"
- Is there something you're sorry for? Write it down, and then write what they might have said back.
- Is there something you're angry about? Anger is part of grief too. Let it onto the page.
- What would you want them to know about how you're doing right now?

## Carrying it forward

- What part of them do you see in yourself?
- What would they say if they saw you today?
- What is one thing you'd like to do this year in their honour?
- How has your idea of what matters changed since they died?
- Write about a moment, even a short one, when you felt something other than sad.
- What do you need from the people around you that you haven't asked for yet?
- Picture yourself a year from now. What do you hope you'll still remember?
- Finish this sentence as many ways as you can: "I am still…"
