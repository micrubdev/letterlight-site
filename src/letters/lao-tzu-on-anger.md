To the one who burns,

You say your temper rises before you can stop it. A careless driver, a sharp word from your partner, a child who will not listen, and suddenly you are shouting. Afterwards you feel ashamed. You ask how to hold it down.

Do not hold it down. A pot with the lid held down boils over.

Look at the angry man. His jaw is tight, his hands are fists, his voice is loud. He believes he is strong. But hardness is the mark of what is about to break. A green branch bends in the storm. A dry branch snaps. Living things are soft and supple. Dead things are stiff. When you are angry, you are making yourself stiff.

Look at water instead. It is the softest thing there is. It does not argue with the rock. It goes around. It goes under. It waits. And in the end the rock is worn smooth and the water is still water. Nothing is better at overcoming what is hard, and yet water never fights.

You think anger protects you. Ask yourself what it has won. Did shouting at the driver teach him to drive? Did the sharp reply make your partner kinder? Did the child listen better, or only more fearfully? The skilled warrior does not rage. He is too busy seeing clearly. Rage is what we do when we have stopped seeing.

So when the heat rises, do less.

Do not speak yet. The words you say in the first minute are the words you will apologise for in the second hour. Let the first minute pass in silence. Breathe out slowly, longer than you breathe in. Unclench your hands. Feel your feet on the floor. You do not have to do anything. Muddy water clears when it is left alone.

Then ask: what is actually happening? Not the story in your head. The thing itself. A car cut in front of you. That is all. The rest, that the driver is selfish, that people never respect you, that the whole world is against you, you added. Most of what burns is the adding.

Step back. A man who is pushed and pushes back has become part of the push. A man who steps aside lets the force go past him. This is not weakness. It is knowing where the fight is not worth having, which is almost everywhere.

Some anger carries a message. Someone has crossed a line. Something unjust is happening. Do not throw the message away with the anger. Wait until you are calm, then say what needs saying, plainly and quietly. A quiet voice is heard further than a loud one, because people stop defending themselves and start listening.

Notice also when anger visits. Often it comes when you are hungry, tired or have taken on too much. You fill your days to the brim and then wonder why they spill. Empty some of the cup. Sleep. Eat. Say no to one thing. A rested person has a longer fuse.

Do not expect to become calm all at once. Water does not carve a valley in a day. Each time you pause before speaking, you wear the habit down a little. Each time you fail, begin again without punishing yourself. Anger at your own anger is just more fire.

If your temper has frightened the people you live with, or led you to hurt someone, get help from someone trained in it. A river needs banks.

Tonight, try one thing. When something irritates you, before you answer, take three slow breaths and soften your hands. That is all. See what happens to the heat when it has nothing to push against.

The softest thing in the world wears down the hardest.
