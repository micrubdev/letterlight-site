To the one who waits,

You say there is a task you keep putting off. Each morning you mean to start. Each evening it is still there, a little heavier. You tidy, you scroll, you answer small messages, and the large thing sits in the corner watching you.

You think the task is too big. It is not too big. You are looking at all of it at once.

No one climbs a mountain. They take a step, and then another. A great tree was once a sprout you could crush between two fingers. A tower of nine storeys began as a small heap of earth. The journey of a thousand li began with one step, and that step was not heroic. It was ordinary. It was a foot lifted and set down.

So do not begin the task. Begin the first step of it. Open the file. Write one sentence. Put on your shoes. Make the phone call's first word. This is so small that your fear will not notice it.

Why do we put things off? Often because we want to do them perfectly. We hold the whole finished work in our mind, shining, and every real beginning looks clumsy beside it. So we wait for a better day, a clearer head, more time. The better day does not come. Muddy water does not become clear by being stirred. Stop stirring. Begin badly. The clumsy start is the only start there is.

Sometimes we put things off because we are already full. A cup filled to the brim cannot take one more drop. If your days are crowded with small tasks, the large one has no room to enter. Empty the cup. Let a few small things go undone. They will not be missed as much as you fear.

Sometimes we put things off because the task is not ours. We do it to please others, or because we think we should, and some part of us refuses. Listen to that part. Ask whether the task must be done at all. Some things are best left alone. Others must be done, and then the refusing part needs a gentler way in.

The sage deals with hard things while they are still easy, and with great things while they are still small. A problem left alone grows. The letter you do not answer becomes an apology you must write. The pain you do not treat becomes an illness. Each day you wait, the first step costs more. Each day you take it, the next step costs less.

Do not fight yourself. You cannot beat a habit by force, any more than you can push a river uphill. Water finds the low path. Make the first step low. Put the book on your pillow. Lay out the running clothes the night before. Leave the document open on the screen. Let the easy way and the right way be the same way.

And when you do start, stop while it is still going well. Leave the work warm, so that tomorrow it is easy to return. The one who works until he is exhausted dreads the next day. The one who stops while he still has strength is glad to come back.

You will fail some days. The river does not apologise for bending. Begin again without scolding yourself. Scolding is only another way of not starting.

Tomorrow morning, before anything else, take one step toward the thing you have been avoiding. Five minutes. Then stop, if you wish, and notice that you are no longer standing still.

The longest road is walked one step at a time.
