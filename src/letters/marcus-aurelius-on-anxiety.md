To you, who cannot sleep.

You write that your mind will not stop. That you lie awake running through everything that might go wrong: the meeting, the test results, the money, your children, your own heart beating too fast. That during the day it hums underneath everything, so that even good moments feel like they are happening behind glass.

I know this. You may imagine that an emperor has nothing to fear, but I spent most of my reign in a tent on the frontier, with plague in the cities behind me and enemies in front, and I was never sure from one month to the next which would come first. I wrote notes to myself in the evenings, not because I had mastered fear, but because I had not. Let me share what helped.

First, notice where your worry lives. It almost never lives in the present. It lives in a future that has not arrived: a conversation next week, a diagnosis that has not been given, a disaster that exists only as a picture in your mind. You are trying to fight battles that are not yet being fought, on ground you cannot see, with forces that may never appear. No general could win like that. No person could either.

I reminded myself often that when the future comes, I will meet it with the same mind I have now. The same reason, the same judgment, the same ability to act. I do not have to borrow tomorrow's trouble today, because tomorrow I will be there to deal with it, and I will not be helpless. Neither will you. You have met hard things before. You are still here.

Second, shrink the field. When the worry is very loud, ask yourself: what is in front of me right now, in this hour? Usually the answer is ordinary. A cup of tea. A task on a screen. A child who needs dinner. Do that one thing, and do it as if it were the only thing in the world. Not perfectly, just fully. The mind cannot be in two places at once. If you give it the present, it has less room for the imagined future.

Third, separate what is yours from what is not. You cannot decide what the doctor will say. You can decide to go to the appointment, ask your questions clearly, and treat the nurse kindly. You cannot control whether the company does well. You can control the quality of today's work. Most of what frightens you sits on the side of things you do not control. Leave it there. I do not say this is easy. I say it is the only place where effort makes any difference.

Fourth, be patient with your body. Fear lives in the chest and the stomach as much as in the thoughts. Breathe more slowly than feels natural. Walk. Let the body learn, again and again, that it is safe in this moment, even if the mind insists it is not.

And if the worry grows so large that it rules your days, if you cannot work or eat or rest, then go to a physician as you would for any other illness. I relied on my doctor Galen more than once. There is no shame in letting a skilled person help you carry something heavy.

Tonight, when the thoughts start, try saying to yourself: that is not happening now. Now I am lying in a bed, and I am breathing, and nothing is required of me until morning. Then let the morning take care of itself. It always arrives, and you will be there to meet it.
