Dear friend,

You say that your mind is like a monkey in a tree, leaping from branch to branch, never still. Worry about money, then about your health, then about something you said three days ago, then back to money. You ask whether there is a way to make it stop.

I will not promise you a mind that never worries. I have not met such a mind, and I have met many people. But I can show you a way to stand beside your worry instead of inside it, and that changes more than you might expect.

Begin with something simple. Right now, as you read this, notice that you are breathing. You did not have to arrange it. The breath comes in, the breath goes out. Feel the air at the tip of your nose, or the rise of your belly. Just one breath. Then another.

There. For those two breaths, where was the worry? Perhaps it was still there, at the edge. But for a moment you were somewhere else: here, in the body, in this moment, which is the only moment there has ever been.

This is not a trick. It is a doorway. The anxious mind lives almost entirely in what has not happened yet. The breath lives only in what is happening now. Each time you return to the breath, you step out of the imagined future and into the real present. You will be pulled back into worry a hundred times a day. That is fine. Return a hundred and one times.

Now look at the worry itself, gently, the way you might look at a cloud. Notice what it is made of. Usually it is a story: if this happens, then that will happen, and then I will be ruined. The story feels like the truth because we have told it so often. But it is a story. The thoughts we repeat shape the mind that repeats them, so it matters which ones we feed. You do not need to argue with the anxious thought or push it away. Simply see it clearly. "Here is a thought about money. Here is a thought about illness." Named in this way, it loses some of its grip. It becomes something you have, rather than something you are.

You will notice that the mind leans in two directions. It reaches for what it wants and pushes away what it fears. Much anxiety comes from pushing. We tense against the bad feeling, and the tension itself becomes a second suffering on top of the first. Try, just for a moment, not pushing. Let the fear be in the body. Breathe around it, as water flows around a stone. You may find it is smaller than it seemed when you were fighting it.

Be kind to yourself in this. Many people, when they notice they are anxious, become angry with themselves for being anxious. Now there are two problems instead of one. Speak to your frightened mind as you would speak to a frightened friend. "I know. This is hard. I am here." Kindness lets the body soften enough to rest.

Walk the middle way in daily life, too. Not too much coffee, not too little sleep. Not endless reading of bad news, not hiding from the world entirely. Eat simply. Move your body. Spend time with people who are calm. These small things matter more than they seem.

And if the worry grows so heavy that it keeps you from living, from working, sleeping or eating, go to a skilled healer. A doctor or a counsellor can help you in ways a letter cannot. There is no shame in seeking help. Even I had teachers.

Tonight, before sleep, try this. Lie on your back. Feel the bed holding you. Follow ten breaths, counting each one. If you lose count, start again at one, without scolding. That is the whole practice. Simple, and not easy, and worth beginning again each night.

May you be safe. May you be at ease. May you be free from fear.
