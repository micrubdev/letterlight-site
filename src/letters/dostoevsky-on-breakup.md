My dear friend,

Your letter moved me more than I can easily say. You loved someone, and they have chosen another life without you. You write that you go over every conversation, looking for the moment it went wrong. You write that you hate them, and in the next line that you would take them back tomorrow. You are ashamed of both feelings.

Do not be ashamed. A person is not a simple thing. We are full of contradictions, and love brings every one of them to the surface at once. You can wish someone well and wish they would suffer a little. You can be relieved and devastated in the same hour. This does not make you wicked or weak. It makes you human, and I have spent my life writing about humans precisely because they are like this.

I will tell you something about myself. In Siberia, after my years in prison, I loved a widow named Maria with all the foolish intensity I was capable of, and for a time she turned toward a younger man. I believed I would die of it. I wrote her long, desperate letters and pleaded my case to anyone who would listen. I was miserable and, I see now, also very much alive. Years before that I had written a story about a lonely dreamer in Petersburg who loves a girl for four white nights and then loses her to another. I thought I was inventing him. Later I understood I had been describing myself.

Here is what I learned, slowly. Love that ends is not love wasted. You were changed by it. You became, for a time, more tender, more attentive, braver than you knew how to be. That person you were in love was you too. You have not lost them, even if you have lost the one you loved.

So I ask you, gently, to stop hunting for the moment it all went wrong. You will not find it, because it was never one moment. It was two people, each with their own wounds, doing their best and sometimes their worst. Going over it a hundred times will not change the ending. It will only keep you standing at the door of a house you no longer live in.

Instead, try something that will feel strange at first. Remember the good days on purpose. Not to torture yourself, but to honour them. Write down one happy memory and let yourself feel it fully, without adding "and now it's gone" at the end. That happiness was real. It happened. Nothing that comes after can make it unhappen.

As for hatred: let it come and let it go, but do not feed it. Bitterness hurts the one who carries it far more than the one it is aimed at. You need not forgive them today. Only stop adding fuel to the fire, and in time it will burn low.

Watch out, too, for the pride that suffers alone. I know it well. It says: no one understands, no one could understand, I will bear this in silence. It is a lie, and a lonely one. Tell a friend the whole thing, even the parts that make you look foolish. Let someone sit with you. Suffering shared becomes bearable. Suffering hidden turns sour.

And do something kind for another person this week, however small. When our own heart is breaking, we feel that the whole world has shrunk down to our pain. Helping someone else lets the world grow large again. I have seen men lift out of the deepest despair simply because a child needed them, or a neighbour asked for help with a heavy load.

If the sadness becomes so heavy that you cannot work or eat or find any reason to get up, please see a doctor. There is no weakness in that. The soul and the body are closer than we think, and both sometimes need care.

You will love again. I do not say this lightly, and I do not say it soon. But the capacity that made this loss so painful is still in you, untouched, waiting. Every person is worthy of love, and that includes you, even now, even in this state, perhaps especially now.
