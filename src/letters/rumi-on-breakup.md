My dear heart,

You write that the one you loved has gone, and that the house feels too large and too quiet. You reach for your phone a dozen times a day to tell them something, and then remember. You ask me how long this will hurt and how to make it stop.

I cannot tell you how long. Love does not keep a calendar. But I can tell you that the hurt is not your enemy, even though it feels like one.

I had a friend once, Shams, who changed everything in me, and then he was gone. I went looking for him. I know what it is to turn at every footstep in the street. The ache in your chest right now is proof that you loved, and loved with your whole self. A heart that has never been opened cannot be broken. Yours was opened. That was brave.

Do not rush to close it again.

I know the temptation. You want to harden, to say "never again," to decide that love is a trick and you were a fool. Many people do this, and they are safe afterwards, and they are also a little dead. The heart that closes to pain also closes to joy. They come in through the same door.

So let the grief have its hours. On some days it will be weather, loud and everywhere, and you will get nothing done. On others it will be a small stone in your shoe. Cry on the loud days. Walk on the quiet ones. You do not have to understand it while it is happening.

What it teaches you, you will know only later. Perhaps you will learn what you need from a partner, and what you will not accept again. Perhaps you will find parts of yourself you set aside to make room for them: a friend you stopped seeing, a song you stopped singing, a city you always meant to visit. Perhaps you will learn that you can survive this, which is no small thing to know.

Here is something you can do in the evenings, when it is hardest. Light a candle, or sit by a window. Put your hand on your chest and feel your heart beating. Say to it, kindly, as you would to a crying child: "I know. You loved, and it hurts." Then breathe with it for a while. You do not need to fix anything. You only need to stay.

During the day, move your body. Walk until your legs are tired. Dance in the kitchen, badly, if a song comes on. Grief gets stuck when we sit still too long. Let it move through you.

Stop checking their pages. I know you want to. Every look is like pressing on a bruise to see if it still hurts. It does. Let it heal instead.

And turn toward the people who love you. Call the friend who makes you laugh. Let your family feed you. You were never meant to carry this alone. Love left through one door, but it is still coming in through many others, if you let it.

One day, and I cannot say when, you will hear a song you both loved and find that it makes you smile instead of weep. You will think of them with tenderness and no longer with pain. You will notice that your heart is larger than it was before, with more room in it, not less.

This ending is not the end of your story with love. Eat something warm tonight, leave the phone in another room, and be gentle with your heart while it mends.
