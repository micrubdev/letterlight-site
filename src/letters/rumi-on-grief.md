Beloved,

You tell me that since she died the house has gone quiet in a new way. Not the quiet of an empty room, you say, but the quiet of a room that is waiting for someone who will not come. I know that quiet. Sit down with me a while. I am not going to hurry you out of it.

Have you ever listened to a reed flute? It is made from a reed that was cut from the riverbank where it grew. Its sound is so sweet and so sad because it is always crying for the river. Every note it plays is a note of longing. And here is the strange thing: if it did not long, it would not sing at all.

Your heart has become such a flute. It was cut from her, and now it cries. Do not think the crying means something has gone wrong in you. It means something went very right. You loved, and love has a sound when it is parted from what it loves. The ache you feel is the shape of her, pressed into you. Who would want that shape to vanish overnight?

People will tell you that time heals. Perhaps. But I would rather say that time teaches the heart to carry what it cannot put down. The weight does not disappear. You grow arms strong enough to hold it. Some mornings you will forget for a moment, and then remember, and it will be as if she died again. That is not going backwards. That is the road. It goes in circles before it goes forward.

There is a guest house inside each of us, and grief has come to stay. Do not bar the door against it. Let it in. Let it sit at your table, even when it breaks the dishes. Sorrow sweeps the house clean in ways we do not understand at the time. It clears out what does not matter, so that what does matter has room. Later, much later, you may find that joy has slipped in through the same door, and that it does not ask you to forget her in order to enter.

Talk to her if you want to. Out loud, in the kitchen, on your walks. Tell her about your day. Keep her coat on its hook by the door a little longer, if you like. Some people will find this foolish. Let them. The conversation between two people who love each other does not end at the edge of a grave. It changes its language, that is all. You may find you hear her answers in your own voice, in what you now choose to do because she would have chosen it.

And do not grieve only alone, in the dark. Grief wants company the way a fire wants air. Find the others who loved her and remember her together. Laugh at the stories, even if the laughter turns into tears halfway through. That is how the reed and the river meet again, for a moment, in the music.

If the darkness grows so thick you cannot see your own hands, reach for someone who can help you find the door. A friend, a doctor, a voice on the end of a line. The heart was never meant to beat alone, and asking is how it remembers.

I will leave you with this. The pain you feel is the price of the love you gave, and you gave a great deal. Let the flute sing. One day you will hear, under the sorrow, that the song was always a love song.
