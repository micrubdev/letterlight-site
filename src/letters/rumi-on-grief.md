Beloved,

You tell me that since she died the house has gone quiet in a new way. Not the quiet of an empty room, you say, but the quiet of a room that is waiting for someone who will not come. I know that quiet. Sit down with me a while. I am not going to hurry you out of it.

When someone dies, people expect the love to stop at the same hour. It does not. It gets up in the morning as it always did and looks for her. It reaches for a second cup. All day it saves up a small story to tell her, and in the evening it has nowhere to put the story down. I think that is much of what you are feeling. Your love has kept its habits, and the person they were for is gone.

So do not think the crying is a fault in you. You loved, and this is how love behaves when it is parted from what it loves. The ache you feel is the shape of her, pressed into you. Who would want that shape to vanish overnight?

People will tell you that time heals. Perhaps. But I would rather say that time teaches the heart to carry what it cannot put down. The weight does not disappear. You grow arms strong enough to hold it. Some mornings you will forget for a moment, and then remember, and it will be as if she died again. That is not going backwards. That is the road. It goes in circles before it goes forward.

Some days will ask little of you, and some will ask for everything you have. On the hard ones, make the day small. Wash one cup. Stand at the window until the light changes. Eat something, even if it has no taste. Nobody is measuring how well you do this. And when an easy hour comes, do not push it away out of loyalty. If something makes you laugh, laugh. You can laugh and miss her in the same breath, and she is no less loved for it.

Talk to her if you want to. Out loud, in the kitchen, on your walks. Tell her about your day. Keep her coat on its hook by the door a little longer, if you like. Some people will find this foolish. Let them. The conversation between two people who love each other does not end at the edge of a grave. It changes its language, that is all. You may find you hear her answers in your own voice, in what you now choose to do because she would have chosen it.

And do not grieve only alone, in the dark. Grief wants company the way a fire wants air. Find the others who loved her and remember her together. Laugh at the stories, even if the laughter turns into tears halfway through. For the length of a story she is in the room again, and everyone there knows it.

If the darkness grows so thick you cannot see your own hands, reach for someone who can help you find the door. A friend, a doctor, a voice on the end of a line. The heart was never meant to beat alone, and asking is how it remembers.

I will leave you with this. It hurts this much because you gave her a great deal of love, and her death does not take any of it back. Go slowly. One day you will say her name aloud and notice that gladness has come along with the tears.
