Dear friend,

Six weeks have passed, you say, and you still sleep on your own side of the bed. You check whether they have been online. You go back through old messages looking for the place where it went wrong, as if finding it would let you return there and choose differently. And you are ashamed, because everyone tells you it is time to let go.

Nobody can let go by being told to. A hand that has held something tightly for years has grown into that shape. It opens slowly, and it aches as it opens.

So leave the letting go for now, and begin by looking.

What is here, this evening, in the room where you are reading? You may find a heaviness in the chest and a tightness in the throat. There is a picture of a face. After the picture comes a story: I will always be alone, I was not enough, they are happy tonight. See how quickly one follows another. The heaviness is painful, and that pain belongs to anyone who has loved. The story comes a moment later and presses on the same bruise. You cannot prevent the bruise. With practice, you can press on it less.

When the next wave comes, try this. Put down the phone. Sit, or stand if you are at work, and feel one breath all the way in and all the way out. Say quietly, "Longing is here." You do not need to send it away, or to feed it by opening the old photographs. Watch it as you would watch rain from a doorway. You will notice something you may not have believed: it grows, it reaches a height, and it lessens. It will come again. But you have now seen with your own eyes that it ends, and what you see for yourself will help you more than anything I could tell you.

In my own youth I went to extremes. I had lived in comfort, and then I starved myself, believing that if I was harsh enough with my body, I would be free of wanting. I became very thin and very weak, and I was no wiser. Finally I ate some food, sat down under a tree, and looked for a path between the two. I think of this when people whose hearts are broken write to me. Some feed the longing all day. Others starve their hearts and say they will never love anyone again. Neither is freedom. Eat your meals, see your friends, weep when you need to weep, and do not make any vows about the rest of your life while you are hungry and tired.

There is a question under your letter that you did not quite ask. If everything changes, was it foolish to love? No. A flower that lasts four days is not a mistake. The suffering came when some part of you required it to last, and nearly everyone makes that demand, so be gentle about having made it. You loved a person, who was changing every day, as you were. For a while your changing went side by side. Now it does not. Nothing that was good in those years is made false by their ending.

You also ask whether you must forgive. Not this week. Begin nearer to home. Place a hand where it hurts and wish yourself well, in plain words: may I be safe, may I rest, may this become easier. If the words feel hollow, say them anyway, as you would speak to a crying child who does not yet believe you. Some weeks from now you may find you can extend the same wish to a stranger. After that, on a quiet day, try it with the one who left. If your heart closes, go back to yourself and begin again. There is no hurry in this.

As for the small things, they matter. Sleep in the middle of the bed. Stop looking to see when they were last online, for one evening first, and then for two. Cook something with a smell that fills the room. Walk the way you always walked together, once, alone, and notice what is there that you never saw while you were talking.

If you find that you cannot eat or rise from bed, or that you no longer wish to live, tell someone today, a doctor or a friend who will sit with you. Good companions are part of the path.

The hand is already opening a little. It opened enough to write to me.
