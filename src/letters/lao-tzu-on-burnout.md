To the one who is tired of trying,

You write that you have given everything and have nothing left. You push and push, and the work grows faster than you can finish it. You rest for a day and feel guilty the whole time. You ask how to find more strength.

I will not help you find more strength. You have tried that already, and look where it has brought you.

Watch a river for a while. It does not try to reach the sea. It goes around the rock instead of breaking itself against it. It moves slowly where the ground is flat and quickly where the ground falls away. It never hurries and it never stops, and in time it wears down mountains. The river is soft, and the softness is what makes it last.

You have been trying to be hard. Hard people break.

Think of a bow that is always strung. It loses its spring. Think of a blade that you keep sharpening. It grows thin and snaps. A field that is planted every season without rest gives poorer crops each year. Everything that lives has a rhythm of doing and not doing. Only people forget this, and then they wonder why they are empty.

You say you cannot stop because so much depends on you. Perhaps. But look closely. Much of what you carry was never yours. You picked it up because no one else did, or because you were afraid of what would happen if it fell. Some of it would fall and nothing bad would happen at all. Some of it others would pick up if you put it down. You will not know which is which until you open your hands.

The sage does less and less, until there is nothing left that is forced. This is not idleness. He moves the way water moves: doing what needs doing, at the right time, with no wasted motion, and then resting. The farmer does not pull on the young plants to make them grow faster. He waters them and waits. Can you water your work and wait?

Here is something to practise. Each morning, before you begin, sit for the time it takes to drink a cup of tea. Do nothing else. Do not plan the day while you drink. Just drink the tea. If this feels impossible, notice that. A person who cannot sit still for one cup of tea is not working by choice anymore.

Then, during the day, look for the places where you strain. The clenched jaw, the held breath, the shoulders raised toward the ears. These are the body telling you that you are pushing against the grain. Soften there. Let the breath go down into the belly. Then do the next small thing, and only that.

At night, stop when the work is good enough, not when it is perfect. Perfection is a hunger that grows the more you feed it. The one who knows when to stop is never in danger.

You may worry that if you do less, people will think less of you. Some will. Let them. Those who praise you for exhausting yourself are not your friends in this. The ones who love you would rather see you whole.

And if you are so empty that rest does not fill you, if you cannot sleep, cannot eat, cannot find any joy in anything, then go to a healer. Sometimes the well is so dry that it needs help to fill again. There is no shame in this. Even the oldest tree leans on the earth.

The world will not end if you step back. The sun rises without your help. The seasons turn without your effort. You are allowed to be part of this, rather than holding all of it up.

Empty your cup a little. Then there is room for something new to come in.
