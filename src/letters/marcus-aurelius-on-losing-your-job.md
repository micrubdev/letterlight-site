To you, from a man who also had his work decided for him.

I did not choose my position. It was handed to me, and for most of my life I would have given it back if I could. So I understand something of what it means when other people decide what your days will be. Yesterday someone decided you would no longer do the work you did. I want to write to you about what they could decide and what they could not.

They could take the title, the salary, the desk by the window, the people you ate with. These were never fully yours. They came from outside, through other people's choices, and what comes in that way can leave the same way. I do not say this to make the loss smaller. I say it so that you see clearly where the loss ends.

Because it does end somewhere. They could not take the care you put into the work, the skill you built over years, the way you treated the junior colleague who was struggling. They could not take your judgment, your patience, or your willingness to begin again tomorrow. Those live in you, not in the company's records. Nobody can dismiss you from them.

I used to write notes to myself in the evenings, in camp, when the day had gone badly. One thing I came back to often was this: the mind can turn what blocks it into material. The obstacle on the road becomes part of the road. I did not always believe it when I wrote it. But I found it was true more often than not, if I was willing to look at the obstacle long enough to see what it asked of me.

So look at this one. What does it ask of you? Perhaps it asks you to stop telling yourself you will change course "next year". Perhaps it asks you to learn the thing you kept postponing. Perhaps it only asks you to get up, wash, dress as if you were going somewhere, and send three honest letters to people who might need what you can do. That is enough for a day. Do not demand more of yourself than a day.

There will be anger. Let it come and let it go. You may decide that the people who made this decision were foolish or unkind, and you may be right. But they are also people, doing what seemed best to them with what they knew, and before long all of us, they and you and I, will be forgotten. I do not say that to make you sad. I find it a relief. It means this need not be the story you tell about yourself for the rest of your life. It is one season.

Watch also for the opposite mistake, which is to treat the loss as a judgment on your worth. A storm that sinks a ship is not a verdict on the captain. Ask what you could have done better, by all means, and write it down honestly. Then close the notebook. Going over your faults every night will not bring the job back, and it will rob you of the sleep you need to find the next one.

In the mornings, when you do not want to rise, remind yourself what you are rising for. Not for a company. For the work of a human being: to be useful, to be honest, to be kind to the people in your house who are worried about you and trying not to show it. That work has no employer. It cannot be cut from a budget. It is waiting for you tomorrow at the same hour as always.

When the next door opens, and it will, walk through it as the same person who walked out of the last one. That person is still here, and still capable.

Keep to your duty. The rest is weather.
