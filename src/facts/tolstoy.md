facts:
  - title: "A night of gambling cost him a novel"
    text: "Stopping in Moscow on a journey, he ran up such losses in one night of high play that he sold the manuscript of The Cossacks to a magazine for £100 to pay what he owed."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Tolstoy"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Tolstoy,_Leo"
    evidence: "one night’s high play cost him the MSS. of The Cossacks"
  - title: "The house he was born in was carted away"
    text: "The grand house where Tolstoy was born was sold to pay some of his earlier gambling debts. It was taken away whole and put up again on another estate about 30 miles off."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Tolstoy"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Tolstoy,_Leo"
    evidence: "The one in which he was born was sold to pay some earlier gambling debts, and had been removed bodily"
  - title: "He failed an entrance exam, then studied Arabic"
    text: "Wanting to be original, the young Tolstoy applied to study Eastern languages at Kazan. He failed at the first attempt, passed at the second, and took up Arabic and Turkish. They didn't suit him, and he moved on."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Tolstoy"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Tolstoy,_Leo"
    evidence: "finally passing in through the medium of a supplemental examination, he took up Arabic and Turkish"
  - title: "He nearly fought a duel with Turgenev"
    text: "Turgenev was praising the way his daughter's governess had her mend the clothes of poor peasants. Tolstoy disliked that sort of charity and let it show. The quarrel at their friend Fet's house grew so fierce that it almost ended in a duel."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Tolstoy"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Tolstoy,_Leo"
    evidence: "the violent quarrel took place between Turgeniev and Tolstoy which nearly culminated in a duel"
  - title: "His wife copied his proofs all night, and he spoiled them by evening"
    text: "While Anna Karenina was being printed, he scribbled over the proofs until only his wife Sonya could read them. She sat up all night making a clean copy. He took it away for one last look and, his son remembered, rewrote it all again."
    source: "Ilya Tolstoy, Reminiscences of Tolstoy, translated by George Calderon"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Reminiscences_of_Tolstoy
    evidence: "My mother would sit up all night copying the whole thing out afresh."
  - title: "He sent corrections by telegraph"
    text: "Even after the pages had been posted, Tolstoy would remember particular words the next day and telegraph the changes. Several times the rewriting interrupted the printing of Anna Karenina, and sometimes nothing came out for months."
    source: "Ilya Tolstoy, Reminiscences of Tolstoy, translated by George Calderon"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Reminiscences_of_Tolstoy
    evidence: "after posting the proofs, he would remember some particular words next day, and correct them by telegraph"
  - title: "A count learned to make boots"
    text: "For about ten years from 1880 he tried to live like a peasant. He got up early to plough and cut corn, helped widows and orphans bring in their crops, and learned to make boots and shoes. He enjoyed being praised for them."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Tolstoy"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Tolstoy,_Leo"
    evidence: "He also learnt boot and shoe making, and enjoyed being praised for his skill."

Leo Tolstoy gambled, quarrelled and rewrote almost everything. Some of these stories come from his son Ilya, who watched it happen.
