facts:
  - title: "He made a better pencil, then quit"
    text: "His father made pencils, and Thoreau set out to make a better one. Chemists and artists in Boston judged it as good as the best London pencils. Friends said his fortune was made. He said he would never make another, because he would not do again what he had done once."
    source: "Ralph Waldo Emerson, Biographical Sketch, in Thoreau's Excursions (1863)"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Excursions_(1863)_Thoreau/Biographical_Sketch
    evidence: "he replied, that he should never"
  - title: "He went to jail rather than pay a tax"
    text: "Thoreau disapproved of some of the ways public money was spent, so he refused to pay his town tax and was put in jail. A friend paid the tax for him, and he was let out."
    source: "Ralph Waldo Emerson, Biographical Sketch, in Thoreau's Excursions (1863)"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Excursions_(1863)_Thoreau/Biographical_Sketch
    evidence: "he refused to pay his town tax, and was put in jail. A friend paid the tax for him"
  - title: "His house at Walden cost $28.12½"
    text: "Thoreau kept accounts of his first months at Walden Pond and printed them in Walden. The house came to 28 dollars and 12½ cents. Food for eight months cost him 8 dollars and 74 cents."
    source: "Henry David Thoreau, Walden (1854), Economy"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Walden_(1854)_Thoreau/Economy
    evidence: "Food eight months"
  - title: "He threw his ornaments out of the window"
    text: "He kept three pieces of limestone on his desk, until he realised they had to be dusted every day. His mind needed dusting more than his furniture did, he decided, and he threw them out of the window."
    source: "Henry David Thoreau, Walden (1854), Economy"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Walden_(1854)_Thoreau/Economy
    evidence: "I had three pieces of limestone on my desk"
  - title: "He found arrowheads everywhere"
    text: "Walking with a stranger who asked where Native American arrowheads could be found, Thoreau said, 'Everywhere.' Then he bent down and picked one up from the ground on the spot."
    source: "Ralph Waldo Emerson, Biographical Sketch, in Thoreau's Excursions (1863)"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Excursions_(1863)_Thoreau/Biographical_Sketch
    evidence: "stooping forward, picked one on the instant from the ground"
  - title: "His favourite dish was the nearest one"
    text: "Thoreau ate no meat, drank no wine and never used tobacco. Asked at dinner which dish he preferred, he answered, 'The nearest.'"
    source: "Ralph Waldo Emerson, Biographical Sketch, in Thoreau's Excursions (1863)"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Excursions_(1863)_Thoreau/Biographical_Sketch
    evidence: "he ate no flesh, he drank no wine, he never knew the use of tobacco"
  - title: "He earned his living as a surveyor"
    text: "He knew the land around Concord so well that he drifted into surveying. Emerson says he liked the work because it kept taking him to new, out-of-the-way places, which helped his study of nature, and he got all the jobs he wanted."
    source: "Ralph Waldo Emerson, Biographical Sketch, in Thoreau's Excursions (1863)"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Excursions_(1863)_Thoreau/Biographical_Sketch
    evidence: "made him drift into the profession of land-surveyor"
---
Henry David Thoreau spent two years in a cabin he built at Walden Pond and wrote a book about it. Several of these stories come from his friend Ralph Waldo Emerson, who wrote about him after his death.
