title: "7 true stories about Epictetus, and one legend"
description: "True stories and facts about Epictetus: his lost name, the beard he refused to shave, a famous clay lamp, and one legend, each with its source."
date: 2026-11-25
facts:
  - title: "We don't know his real name"
    text: "Epictetus is the Greek word for \"acquired\". It is the only name we have for him, and whatever he was called before has been lost."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Epictetus"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Epictetus
    evidence: "his original name is not known"
  - title: "He belonged to a man at Nero's court"
    text: "As a boy he was a slave in the house of Epaphroditus, a freedman who served the emperor Nero. He still managed to attend the lectures of the Stoic teacher Musonius Rufus, and was later freed."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Epictetus"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Epictetus
    evidence: "As a boy he was a slave in the house of Epaphroditus, a freedman and courtier of the emperor Nero."
  - title: "His teacher told him off over a logic puzzle"
    text: "Musonius Rufus scolded him for missing a step in a logical argument. Epictetus protested that he hadn't burnt down the Capitol. The missing step, Musonius replied, was the Capitol here. Epictetus told the story against himself when he became a teacher."
    source: "Epictetus, Discourses, Book I, translated by George Long"
    source_url: https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/discourses.1.one.html
    evidence: "This is the very remark which I made to Rufus when he blamed me for not having discovered the one thing omitted in a certain syllogism"
  - title: "He would rather lose his head than his beard"
    text: "A beard marked out a philosopher, and he imagined someone ordering him to shave. A philosopher would refuse, he answered. And if they threatened to take off his head? If that does you any good, he said, take it."
    source: "Epictetus, Discourses, Book I, translated by George Long"
    source_url: https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/discourses.1.one.html
    evidence: "\"But I will take off your head?\" If that will do you any good, take it off."
  - title: "He moved to a town full of earthquakes"
    text: "The emperor Domitian expelled the philosophers from Rome, and Epictetus settled at Nicopolis in Greece. Answering someone who feared for his life because of Caesar, he pointed out that he himself lived with earthquakes."
    source: "Epictetus, Discourses, Book II, translated by George Long"
    source_url: https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/discourses.2.two.html
    evidence: "And am not I in danger who dwell in Nicopolis, where there are so many earthquakes"
  - title: "He never wrote a book"
    text: "Everything we read under his name was taken down by a pupil, Arrian, who is better known as the historian of Alexander the Great. The short Enchiridion and four books of the Discourses survive."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Epictetus"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Epictetus
    evidence: "He wrote nothing; but much of his teaching was taken down with affectionate care by his pupil Flavius Arrianus"
  - title: "His clay lamp sold for a fortune"
    text: "The writer Lucian says that the plain earthenware lamp Epictetus had owned was bought by a collector for 3,000 drachmas. It is an odd end for the lamp of a man who taught people not to be attached to things."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Epictetus"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Epictetus
    evidence: "According to Lucian, the earthenware lamp which had belonged to the sage was bought by an antiquarian for 3000 drachmas."
  - title: "Did his master break his leg?"
    status: legend
    text: "A popular story says his master twisted his leg until it snapped, while Epictetus calmly warned him that it would. The source below says only that he was lame and in weak health, so we treat the story as a legend."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Epictetus"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Epictetus
    evidence: "He was lame and of weakly health."
---
Epictetus began life as a slave and ended it as one of the most respected teachers in the Roman world. These are some of the stories behind the advice.
