title: "5 facts and 2 legends about the Buddha"
description: "Facts and legends about the Buddha: born on a journey, six years of fasting, a scholar who called him a myth, and his last meal, with each source given."
date: 2026-11-26
facts:
  - title: "He was born on the way to his grandparents"
    text: "His mother set out to give birth at her parents' home. The party stopped in a garden called Lumbini, and her son was born there unexpectedly. The site was found again in modern times, marked by a pillar put up by the emperor Asoka."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Buddha"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Buddha
    evidence: "marked by an inscribed pillar put up by Asoka"
  - title: "Almost nothing is known of his youth"
    text: "His family name was Gotama and his own name Siddhattha. At nineteen he married his cousin Yasodhara and lived in luxury. The 1911 Britannica calls this the only record of his youth."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Buddha"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Buddha
    evidence: "This is the solitary record of his youth"
  - title: "Fasting nearly killed him"
    text: "For six years he practised the harshest self-denial and became famous for it. One day, weak with hunger, he collapsed, and some thought he was dead. When he recovered he began eating normally again, and his five followers lost respect for him and left."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Buddha"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Buddha
    evidence: "from that time took regular food and gave up his severe penance"
  - title: "An Oxford professor said he never existed"
    text: "As late as 1854 Professor Wilson of Oxford told the Royal Asiatic Society that the Buddha was a myth, an imaginary being. By 1911 no one held that view."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Buddha"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Buddha
    evidence: "No one, however, would now support this view"
  - title: "He made sure no one blamed his host"
    text: "At eighty he ate a meal given by a goldsmith named Chunda, and on the road afterwards he grew weak. Feeling that he was dying, he asked his companion Ananda to tell Chunda that the meal was one of the two most blessed gifts he had ever received, so that no one would blame him."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Buddha"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Buddha
    evidence: "careful lest Chunda should be reproached by himself or others"
  - title: "Four sights sent him away from home"
    status: legend
    text: "The story goes that he saw an old man, a sick man and a corpse, and his charioteer told him this was the fate of everyone. Then he saw a calm wandering ascetic. The 1911 Britannica suggests the sights may have happened in his mind rather than in fact."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Buddha"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Buddha
    evidence: "Each time his charioteer, whose name was Channa, told him that such was the fate of all living beings"
  - title: "News of his son came the same day"
    status: legend
    text: "In the later accounts, a messenger came later that day to say his wife had given birth to a son, his only child. He is said to have answered quietly that this was a new and strong tie he would have to break."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Buddha"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Buddha
    evidence: "is a new and strong tie I shall have to break"
---
Most of what is told about the Buddha was written down centuries after his death, and legends gathered around it. These are the parts a scholarly encyclopedia of 1911 accepted, and two stories it marked as legend.
