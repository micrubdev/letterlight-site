facts:
  - title: "His prize essay was on Socrates, and it was dull"
    text: "At Harvard he was about the middle of his class, but he won prizes for speaking and for essays. One essay was on the character of Socrates. The Britannica of 1911 calls both prize essays rather dull and formal."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Emerson"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Emerson,_Ralph_Waldo"
    evidence: "the first essay being on"
  - title: "He taught at a school for young ladies and hated it"
    text: "After college he helped at his brother William's school for young ladies in Boston. He stayed three years, unhappily. The routine bored him, and he resented the small details that took up so much of his time."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Emerson"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Emerson,_Ralph_Waldo"
    evidence: "continued teaching, with much inward reluctance and discomfort, for three years"
  - title: "He gave up his church over one ritual"
    text: "In 1832, the year his first wife died, Emerson decided the Lord's Supper was not meant to be a permanent sacrament. He offered to keep the service as a simple act of remembrance. His congregation did not agree, and he left. He never led a parish again."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Emerson"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Emerson,_Ralph_Waldo"
    evidence: "He never again took charge of a parish"
  - title: "His first lectures were about science"
    text: "Back from Europe in 1833, he began lecturing in Boston. The first talks were given to the Society of Natural History and the Mechanics' Institute, and most were on scientific subjects, though he approached them like a poet."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Emerson"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Emerson,_Ralph_Waldo"
    evidence: "They were chiefly on scientific subjects, approached in a poetic spirit"
  - title: "He and Carlyle published each other"
    text: "On his trip to Europe in 1833 he visited Thomas Carlyle at a lonely Scottish farmhouse, and they stayed friends for life. Emerson published Carlyle's first books in America, and Carlyle brought Emerson's Essays to England."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Emerson"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Emerson,_Ralph_Waldo"
    evidence: "Emerson published Carlyle"
  - title: "An aunt who disagreed with him shaped him"
    text: "His aunt Mary Moody Emerson loved him, believed in him and wanted him to succeed. She also held fast to the old faith he drifted away from. The Britannica says she did as much as anyone to form his mind."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Emerson"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Emerson,_Ralph_Waldo"
    evidence: "was a potent factor in his education"
  - title: "The public paid to rebuild his house"
    text: "In 1872 Emerson's house burned down. It was rebuilt with money raised by public subscription. The same year, nearly seventy, he set off on his third journey abroad and went as far as Egypt."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Emerson"
    source_url: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Emerson,_Ralph_Waldo"
    evidence: "In 1872 his house was burned down, and was rebuilt by popular subscription"
---
Seven of Ralph Waldo Emerson's ancestors were New England ministers, and he trained to be one too. He became famous instead as a lecturer and essayist. These stories come from before and after the fame.
