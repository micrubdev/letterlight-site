facts:
  - title: "His family fled when he was a child"
    text: "Rumi was born in 1207 in Balkh, in Khorasan, in what is now northern Afghanistan. His father, a scholar famous for his learning, feared the jealousy of the sultan and in 1212 took the family west to Asia Minor."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Rumi"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/R%C5%ABm%C4%AB
    evidence: "being afraid of the sultan"
  - title: "His name means 'from Rum'"
    text: "The family settled in Konya, in a land people in the East then called Rum, after Rome. That is where the poet's surname, Rumi, comes from. His own name was Jalal al-Din."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Rumi"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/R%C5%ABm%C4%AB
    evidence: "or, as it is commonly called in the East"
  - title: "He gave up on the exact sciences"
    text: "After his father died in 1231 he went to study in Aleppo and Damascus, but the exact sciences left him dissatisfied. He came back to Konya, taught at four colleges, and turned to mysticism."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Rumi"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/R%C5%ABm%C4%AB
    evidence: "where he became by and by professor of four separate colleges"
  - title: "A wandering stranger changed him"
    text: "A wandering Sufi called Shams of Tabriz soon had a powerful hold on him. Shams's forceful manner turned the people of Konya against him, and after a riot he was never seen again."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Rumi"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/R%C5%ABm%C4%AB
    evidence: "at least he was no more seen"
  - title: "The whirling dance stands for the planets"
    text: "Rumi founded the order of dervishes known in Turkish as the Mevlevi. Their turning dance pictures the circling of the heavenly spheres and, inwardly, a soul moved by love of God. His family led the order for six hundred years."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Rumi"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/R%C5%ABm%C4%AB
    evidence: "the outward representation of the circling movement of the spheres"
  - title: "He dictated his great poem to a disciple"
    text: "The idea of the Masnavi, a poem of 30,000 to 40,000 verses in six books, came from his favourite disciple, Husam al-Din. Rumi dictated it to him over the rest of his life and died soon after finishing it, in 1273."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Rumi"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/R%C5%ABm%C4%AB
    evidence: "dictated to him, with a short interruption, the whole work during the remaining years of his life"
  - title: "A seventh book isn't his"
    text: "Some printed editions add a seventh book to the Masnavi. A note in Rumi's own words, in one of the copies at Oxford's Bodleian Library, shows it isn't genuine."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Rumi"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/R%C5%ABm%C4%AB
    evidence: "the genuineness of which is refuted by a remark of"
