facts:
  - title: "An emperor made a pun on his name"
    text: "He was born Marcus Annius Verus. The boy caught the emperor Hadrian's attention, and since verus means true, he joked that the name ought to be Verissimus: truest. The nickname even turns up on medals."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Marcus Aurelius"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus
    evidence: "playing upon the name “Verus,” said that it should be changed to “Verissimus”"
  - title: "He wanted to sleep on a plank"
    text: "At eleven he met the painter and philosopher Diognetus and began dressing like a Stoic. He later thanked Diognetus for making him want a plank bed covered with a skin, as the Greek philosophers had."
    source: "Marcus Aurelius, Meditations I.6, translated by George Long"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Thoughts_of_the_Emperor_Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus/Book_I
    evidence: "to have desired a plank bed and skin, and whatever else of the kind belongs to the Grecian discipline"
  - title: "He refused to pick a team"
    text: "Romans were passionate about chariot racing and its Green and Blue teams. Marcus thanked his governor, the man in charge of his upbringing, for teaching him to support neither, and to take no side between rival kinds of gladiator."
    source: "Marcus Aurelius, Meditations I.5, translated by George Long"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Thoughts_of_the_Emperor_Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus/Book_I
    evidence: "to be neither of the green nor of the blue party at the games in the Circus"
  - title: "He believed dreams had given him cures"
    text: "In the list of things he was grateful for, he includes remedies shown to him in dreams, among them some for spitting blood and giddiness. He doesn't say what the remedies were."
    source: "Marcus Aurelius, Meditations I.17, translated by George Long"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Thoughts_of_the_Emperor_Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus/Book_I
    evidence: "remedies have been shown to me by dreams, both others, and against bloodspitting and giddiness"
  - title: "He shared the throne when he didn't have to"
    text: "When Antoninus Pius died in 161 he had named only Marcus as his successor, and the senate is believed to have wanted him to rule alone. He made his adoptive brother Lucius Verus his partner. Rome had never had two emperors at once."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Marcus Aurelius"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus
    evidence: "This was the first time that Rome had two emperors"
  - title: "He pardoned a rebel's family"
    text: "In 175 the general Avidius Cassius declared himself emperor and was murdered three months later. Marcus persuaded the senate to pardon his whole family. It is also said that he burned the rebel's letters without reading them."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Marcus Aurelius"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus
    evidence: "persuaded the senate to pardon all the family of Cassius"
  - title: "He didn't call his book Meditations"
    text: "That title is not his. The notes were written when he found a moment, in the middle of public business and on the eve of battles, which is why they jump about. He is believed to have written an autobiography too, now lost."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Marcus Aurelius"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus
    evidence: "in the midst of public business, and on the eve of battles on which the fate of the empire depended"
  - title: "Did a miracle save his army?"
    status: legend
    text: "Trapped and suffering from thirst, the story goes, his soldiers were saved by a sudden storm that gave them rain and battered their enemies with hail. Pagan writers credited an Egyptian magician who called on Mercury and other gods. Christians credited the prayers of Christian soldiers."
    source: "Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), Marcus Aurelius"
    source_url: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus
    evidence: "the Romans, entangled in a defile, were suffering from thirst. A sudden storm gave abundance of rain"
